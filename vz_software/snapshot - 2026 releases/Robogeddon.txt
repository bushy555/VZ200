ROBOGEDDON 2085
The year is 2085, and the machines have turned on us. One family is left alive, wandering an arena full of murderous robots, and only you stand between them and the scrap heap.
Move with one stick and blast with the other. ROBOGEDDON is frantic, arcade-style dual-stick action in the spirit of the 1982 classic. You run one way and fire another, carving paths through swarms of droids while you dive across the arena to pull the last humans to safety. Every wave explodes onto the screen line by line, and every robot you hit shatters apart in a burst of scanlines. There's no time to think, only to move and shoot, and to keep moving.
Twenty waves of mayhem
Droids march straight at you, and the longer you take, the faster they come
Pylons kill on touch
Crushers can't be stopped. Bullets only knock them back, and they flatten any human they catch
Mindbots hunt your family, turn them into twitching Mutants, and launch homing missiles
Orbs bounce around hatching spark-spitting Seekers
Factories roll out Tanks that lob shells off the walls
Save the humans for 1,000 to 5,000 points apiece. Grab 3-Way spread-shot and Shield power-ups, earn the Full Family Bonus, and pick up an extra life every 25,000 points. The first ten waves ease you in gently; from wave 11 the gloves come off.
Play it your way
Dual stick: two VZ joysticks, one to move and one to aim, the true Robotron way
One stick: hold fire to shoot and strafe
Keyboard: W A S D to move, I J K L to shoot

Tech specs
Machine
Dick Smith VZ200 / VZ300 (Z80 at 3.55 MHz), PAL
Needs
BennVenn SD loader + 128K expansion
Display
Mode 1, 128×64, 4 colours: a blue background inside the VZ's green border
Frame rate
Steady 25 fps on normal waves; the busiest waves (50+ robots) mix 25 and 16.7 fps
Snow
None. All video RAM writes happen in vertical blank
Rendering
Double-buffered. An IM2 field-sync interrupt copies each frame to video RAM in two halves, using unrolled POP/PUSH code built at start-up in the card's SRAM. Sprites are pre-shifted and drawn through masks
Objects
Up to 64 robots, humans and effects, plus 12 enemy bullets and 6 player shots
Sound
1-bit beeper: a phase-accumulator square wave and pitched noise; original title tune and effects
Controls
Dual VZ joysticks, one joystick, or keyboard
File
ROBOGEDN.VZ: one 22K type-F1 binary that loads at $8000
Code
100% Z80 assembly. Original code, graphics and music
