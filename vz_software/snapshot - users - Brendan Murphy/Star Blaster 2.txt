STAR BLASTER II – "The Enermy Returns"

Out now for the Dick Smith VZ300 / VZ200: the 1984 VZ cockpit shooter STAR BLASTER gets a sequel.
You're back in the cockpit with the crosshair, the starfield and the FRT TRP TNK FHT tally. Energy is still your life: it drains all the time, every shot costs some, and every hit costs more. But the Enermy now surrounds you:
360° space with a radar. Scan left, right, up and down. The radar strip in the dashboard shows every contact around the full circle.
Twin laser bolts from the cockpit corners, a crosshair that turns red on target, and combo chains worth ×2, ×3, ×4.
Scaled sprites. Ships grow from specks to full size as they close in, at up to 8 sizes each.
Eight sectors, each with its own twist: the Outer Rim, the Rock Belt, the Minefield, the Invasion (stop the troop carriers before they land), Nebula X (cloaked fighters in the alternate palette), Ace Squadron, the Gauntlet, and the Enermy Mothership. That boss has four turrets to knock out before you can hit its core.
A dithered dashboard bezel that does the talking: it flashes when you're hit and blinks on the side a threat is coming from.
Hyperspace warp-ins with star streaks and a fanfare, a refuelling tally after each sector, supply pods, NOVA smart bombs, and a faster lap 2 after the mission is complete.
Presentation:
a Mode 1 title where the logo letters fly in and an Enermy fighter gets blasted
an attract cycle (mission briefing, fleet gallery, controls, a computer-played demo)
a Hall of Fame with initials
a trainer mode
all-new two-voice beeper music and 14 sound effects
VIBED BY BPM 2026.
TECH SPECS
Machine
Dick Smith VZ300 (VZ200 compatible), PAL
Needs
BennVenn SD loader with 128K RAM. Without the expansion RAM and SRAM bank it shows NEEDS BENNVENN SD + 128K RAM and stops.
File
STARBLS2.VZ: a single 20,950-byte type F1 file. It loads at $8000–$D1BD and runs at $8000.
Memory
Uses all 64K plus one 10K SRAM bank (port $37 = 3, written once). See the RAM map below. No ROM calls, and the DOS isn't needed once loaded.
Display
Mode 1, 128×64, 4 colours. Play is in CSS0 (green, yellow, blue, red); Nebula X switches to CSS1.
Frame rate
25 game-logic steps a second, locked to field sync, interrupts off. The picture updates at 25 fps (every 2nd field), about 22 fps on average in the busiest play; late frames are caught up so the game speed never changes.
Sprites
10 ship and object types (including the planet and the mothership) at up to 8 distance sizes each. Only one pixel phase is stored in the file; the other three shifts and the masks (14.5 KB) are generated at start-up. Clipped, masked POP blitter at about 38 T-states a byte.
No snow
Everything is drawn into a back buffer, and the changed byte spans are recorded per row. Before each vblank the game generates a straight-line copy routine (an LDI chain) for those spans, then runs it as field sync starts, within a 12,288 T-state budget.
Sound
1-bit speaker. Two-voice pin-pulse engine, 9 tunes, 14 effects, three modes (music+FX / FX / off).
Controls
Keyboard (M , Q A + Z/SPACE, the original's keys), or either joystick on the VZ joystick interface, detected automatically
Content
8 sectors plus the mothership, a 360° radar, a 3×5 custom font, and a 5-entry high-score table with initials
Source
About 6,700 lines of Z80 (sjasmplus) plus Python generators for the sprites, logo, font and music
Testing
Headless VZ300 emulator with a snow detector and DOS-bank and system-area write counters. A 30,000-frame autoplay soak runs two full laps with 0 snow, 0 DOS-bank writes and 0 system-area writes. Not yet run on real hardware.

Load: copy STARBLS2.VZ to the SD card and run it from the BennVenn loader. SPACE or fire starts the game, and keys 1–5 on the title screen pick the skill level. N fires a NOVA, P pauses and S changes the sound mode.
