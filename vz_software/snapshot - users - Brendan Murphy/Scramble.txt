SCRAMBLE for the VZ300
Scramble comes to the Dick Smith VZ300, converted from the ZX Spectrum release and rebuilt for the BennVenn 128K expansion and SD loader. Fly low over enemy territory through five stages: missile hills, the UFO zone, a meteor storm, a brick-built fortress and finally the enemy base. Destroy the base to complete the mission, then go round again a little faster. Fuel drains as you fly, so you have to bomb fuel tanks to stay in the air. It keeps the Spectrum’s rules and scoring, its three speeds, a choice of starting stage, and a best score with a name for each speed. On top of that it adds a trainer mode with infinite lives, a computer pilot that plays each stage in the attract mode, and a GAME OVER sequence over burning wreckage.
The level itself comes from the Spectrum tape. The original terrain bytes and level script are decoded by a direct port of the Spectrum’s own terrain routine, so every hill, rocket, fuel tank and the base appear exactly where they do on the Spectrum. Everything else was rebuilt for the VZ’s 128×64 four-colour mode. The landscape scrolls smoothly two pixels at a time, where the Spectrum jumps eight, and the slopes are drawn as real slopes rather than blocky character cells. Each stage has its own palette, using both of the VZ’s colour sets so all eight colours appear over a mission. There are dithered rock and brick textures, a far ridge for depth, twinkling parallax stars, new four-colour sprites, and particle explosions with debris, screen flashes and screen shake. Collisions are checked pixel by pixel, where the Spectrum read characters back off the screen.
The sound pushes the VZ’s one-bit speaker further than usual. The title theme is an original A-minor tune for a custom three-voice player modelled on the VZ300 Cybernoid conversion. A bass, a lead with vibrato and arpeggiated chords take turns at the speaker 20,000 times a second, each as a pulse whose width sets its volume, so they blend into one sound. Drums play in the third voice’s turn: a pitch-dropping kick, plus a snare and hi-hat made from noise. The switching happens above the range of hearing, so there’s no whine. The tune is also kept in step through the brief pause each frame while the screen is drawn, so it stays in tune. All of this, including a two-page mode(1) title with sliding lettering over a star field, packs into a single .VZ file of about 11 KB. It unpacks itself at start-up, with no data files to load.
